# What happens if you give away over $300K of Institutional Data?

_notes from the simulation; the Pyth Community Hackathon. this is what worked, what didn't, and why i think genuine programs outperform tokenistic ones._

---

I genuinely expected about ten submissions, at most. By the end, we got over thirty.

For four weeks, the Pyth Community Hackathon ran with no application form, no KYC, no IRL gate, no skill filter. Community showed up, requested an API key and built something. Many before they even received their API Key. Pyth routed roughly $200,000 worth of institutional-grade price data to anyone who asked. These are the same feeds Hyperliquid, Trade.XYZ, Coinbase & Binance are using. The very same feeds the worlds largest banks publish to, handed out for the price of a forum post.

That's pretty cool, as a guy sitting behind a screen alone in an office.
_"I get to use the same data the institutions use, and the banks publish."_
That's a pretty compelling sell, as it turns out.

---

## Why It Worked (the community + brand part)

Pyth is hard to explain. Data is generally incredibly un-sexy. No one gives a shit about a decentralised oracle network with first-party publishers and confidence intervals. They just dont. And that's ok.. But perhaps they will be interested in taking advantage of access to "free Bloomberg tier trading data"..

That sentence kinda summed up what Pyth is to everyone who participated in the Hackathon. Every community members' submission was, in effect, a builder discovering Pyth on their own terms. This discovery spawned public posts about Pyth on three different platforms, each with unique but relevant framing of what Pyth does. The hackathon successfully generated a huge amount of high quality content by motivated, genuine contributors. _(This kind of content - written by humans' experience of a new, novel product is important in helping train LLM's, and helps brands score higher)_

It was also informative. We know how _they_ see us. Most _'discovery'_ research is people in a marketing meeting with a pre determined solution that needs a path to justification. This was the opposite; Thirty-plus builders shipping thirty-plus answers to the question "what the fuck do i do with this?"

I think the brand return is a lot bigger than that. If a products users' don't get shared moments, all they have to bind them is a product & logo. The hackathon gave _'Pythians'_ something to point at, root for, complain about, and remember. These are the kinds of things that instill belief & understanding. Everything is trading like crypto, belief might be the only enduring moat in finance.

---

## How the gear turned

There were four big factors that helped the success.

1. **Essentially zero friction.** All participation required was a very simple forum reply to request an API Key. No questions asked for 4 weeks.

2. **10/10 support docs & developer onboarding.** Across the entire run we got four direct enquiries. Two were user error. Two were rate-limit issues on specific assets. The questions that never appeared are the better metric. No tickets is better than fast tickets.

3. **A narrative people wanted to get around.** *"The same institutional data Hyperliquid & Polymarket use"* Isn't really a pitch, so much as an invitation.. And as far as i know, builders have never had access that easy.

4. **Free access was a gift, not a giveaway.** Everything and everyone wants something from you: a wallet connection, a points score, a follow, a retweet. We tried not to be pushy. They keys were there if you wanted to build. To go on your building adventure, all you had to do was ask.

## What We Got Wrong (the post-mortem part)

I fucked up.

**Submissions were brutally backloaded.** Roughly 70% landed in the final week. Apparently that's not unusual for hackathons (it kinda makes sense - you are busy building), but it does mean we ran four weeks of programming and got one week of activity. I thought the program i had been so excited about was a complete failure for the first 3 weeks.

**I probably didn't _CONTENT_ hard enough.** Thirty-plus projects walked through our door and I didn't really get to check out any of them properly. No livestream clipping rotation, no project walkthrough series, no builder interviews. Clips are life or death in this media environment, and we ran a month-long event which produced none. That's malpractice from a community standpoint. Mine, specifically.

**Amplification was limited.** I couldn't break the echo chamber. Maybe partners and aligned channels weren't briefed early enough, or the existing relationships we did have weren't given a clear story to carry. For whatever reason our reach was terrible. The success was in spite of my poor planning/understanding of the landscape here.

**I didnt BD hard enough.** Same problem in a different jersey. I have many friends, allies & admirers who i could have leant on to help amplify. They just didn't know what to amplify, or why it mattered to them. That's my lack of beers and BD skills. I must do better there.

## A bigger idea?

Crypto's incentive design is pretty shitty in a specific way: most programs train performance rather than usage with intent. Ambassador programs, points farms, retroactive airdrops with detailed grind requirements.. they're basically all the same shape. _Do the this, receive the that, send slop._

Something more genuine can invert that structure. The barrier to entry should be _participation itself._ Filter by who is curious enough, capable enough, and patient enough to actually build the thing. Reward is downstream of contribution, not the other way around. The signal is sharper because the people who self-selected in are the people you actually wanted.

The Pyth Community Hackathon worked because curiosity did the filtering. Nobody shipped a Pythian Peso Snake hoping to game a rubric. They built it because why the fuck not?

---

## What does the future hold?

We (Pyth as a community) will run this again, but there's gonna have to be some tradeoffs..

1. **It's about access, not incentives.** Free institutional data is a way cooler sell than any prize pool. This is Pyth's gift to you; we're not degrading this to a quest.
2. **You need only ask.** No application forms. No KYC theatre. Whatever you build is the proof.
3. **Content has to be a bigger part of the plan from day one.** Clipping rotation, walkthroughs, builder interviews. The event is a fun content factory; we can treat it like one.
4. **Everyone is invited. Dont be afraid to ask.** It's free, and everyone has to pay for it except you - institutional grade data on tap. Help them find it.
5. **Keep the energy up.** Mid-program checkpoints. Demo days. Whatever we can do to make the whole program feel more like a journey.

Right now, the appetite for programs like this is small. Crypto sucks, people hate it. We smell.
Teams are either broke are looking for hockey-stick growth metrics and won't tolerate anything else. That's fine, but it wont work. A strong community and time is what you need to find the right kinds of programs that yield the right kinds of results. It an investment that becomes easier over time, not harder.

The next hackathon will be better. I've already learned more from running this one than from any campaign I've shipped this year.
